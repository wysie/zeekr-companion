## Prolonged missing picture

The evening snapshot contains a clear access-denial/recovery failure:

| Time, 1 November | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| 19:18:35 | Android/main display wakes; app process survives and is in the background. |
| 19:18:36–37 | Surround is briefly occupied elsewhere and then reported free. Scheduler still says camera access is unavailable until the main Activity comes forward. Popup reports the recorded `ERROR_CAMERA_DISABLED (3)` error. This same denial state was already present before this wake. |
| 19:19:16 | Recording is requested, but **0/3** sources record: surround, front and rear all wait for foreground camera access. |
| 19:25–19:33 | Multiple indicator popups receive no frames; recording remains logically active but waiting. |
| 19:33:48–49 | Main Activity recreates/comes forward; surround opens and produces frames in about 0.3 s, followed by the dedicated cameras and recording. |

The initial platform reason for denying camera access is not established by our
app logs. The app-side recovery defect is identifiable: the scheduler remembers
an actual denial (`unusableSince`), while the recording coordinator's recovery
test only reads the foreground service's cached `startedWhileVisible` eligibility.
That older eligibility can remain true across sleep. The coordinator also treats
the zero-live-source WAITING state as a logically active recording, so routine
resume returns early. A camera becoming free cannot clear this access denial;
the current scheduler intentionally requires a foreground event.

The correction exposes the scheduler's denial episode to recording recovery and
checks it when recording state is published/resumed/started. If recording remains
wanted, every source is waiting, and the screen is on, it requests the existing
foreground recovery path once per accepted denial episode. Factory-camera/quit
guards and the existing launch throttle remain. Rejected factory/throttle
requests do not consume the episode. An accepted request is not proof Android
displayed the Activity; manual app opening remains a fallback. No bypass of camera
permissions or repeated blind camera-open loop was added.

This incident is not evidence that the outstanding-close risk in upstream #117
caused the outage, nor a confirmed explanation for App Lab disappearance.

## Black picture while adjusting Super mirror

Source tracing reproduces a separate GPU presentation bug. `SideGpuOutput`
replaced its `SideGlRequest` on every crop/window-size change, cancelling the old
generation. Its fresh-picture test then looked only in the new generation, which
had no acquired frame yet. Continuous gestures could keep replacing it before the
UI acquired a matching new frame; lifting the finger allowed it to settle.

The correction keeps a bounded history of **output-frame metadata** across
framing edits on the same pipe, output SurfaceTexture and camera lane. It accepts
only the timestamp actually acquired by the UI. The original camera-source age
still expires after 500 ms; settings redraws never renew it. Lane, output or pipe
changes reset continuity; request cancellation/failure still blocks drawing.
No pixel-buffer history, camera rebuild, source resolution or recording change.

### USB-only follow-up: write/sync and cleanup trace

The owner already uses a fast USB card reader and does not want internal recording
or relay staging. No storage setting, destination, retention limit or quality was
changed. Reviewed `CodecVideoRecorder`, `SampleRing`, `StorageGuard`, `StoragePlan`,
`StorageCleanupManager`, `FootageLocks`, `ActiveFootage` and the manager callbacks
against the four October 30–November 1 drive sessions.

**The repeated long native writes overlap automatic clip deletion.** Eight
greater-than-three-second write warnings describe three overlapping events, not
eight independent freezes:

| November 1 cleanup window | Cleanup observation | Long writes overlapping it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 16:26:45.943–16:26:51.595 (5.652 s) | Three old clips, about 221.6 MB | Cameras 0/1: 5.040 / 5.039 s |
| 19:35:51.172–19:35:59.577 (8.405 s) | Five old clips, about 409.2 MB | Cameras 0/1/3: 5.645 / 5.648 / 5.711 s |
| 19:37:52.125–19:37:57.177 (5.052 s) | Three old clips, about 227.9 MB | Cameras 0/1/3: 3.747 / 3.757 / 3.751 s |

Windows begin at the logged DELETE decision and end at the deletion summary;
inventory work can start earlier and individual unlink durations are not logged.
The evening thread samples show all three writers in
`MediaMuxer.nativeWriteSampleData`, rather than waiting for an application Java
monitor. Cleanup is a strong filesystem-contention candidate, not proof of the
head unit's kernel/USB/controller behavior.

The app was enforcing a **10 GB video cap** while approximately **228 GB remained
free**. `AppConfig.getVideoStorageLimitGb()` defaults to 10 GB; the cap is separate
from physical free space. `StoragePlan` reserves another estimated segment under
that cap, and a completed segment invokes `checkStorage`. Once that rolling cap
is reached, this can mean deleting another group at successive minute boundaries.
All three streams continue writing while cleanup deletes eligible closed files.
Putting cleanup on a worker keeps Java UI/camera locks independent, but does not
give it a separate physical drive.

**There is also a separate sync-latency problem.** Each recorder posts a periodic
sync five seconds after its writer starts and again five seconds after each sync
returns. Each segment close additionally stops the muxer and syncs the file.
The eight-second surround sync at 16:25:48 started around 16:25:40.260, before
that segment's DELETE decision at 16:25:48.610. Therefore that deletion cannot
explain the start of this sync pause. Other long syncs also have no deletion
decision in their interval. Android's `FileDescriptor.sync()` deliberately waits
for the descriptor's modified buffers to reach storage; it is not a memory-only
operation ([API](https://developer.android.com/reference/java/io/FileDescriptor#sync())).
The exports cannot distinguish card firmware, USB/controller/filesystem latency
or contention within Android's native muxer/storage layer.

The logged encoder targets are 23.9 Mbps surround plus 4 Mbps each for front/rear,
about **4 MB/s aggregate target**. Segment files total roughly 225–229 MB per
minute. Headline reader bandwidth alone therefore does not explain the seconds-long
tail latency, and the logs do not establish a faulty or insufficient reader.

**Why frames drop:** `SampleRing.QUEUE_MS` is 6,000 ms; the independent per-source
byte ceiling is at most 24 MiB. The encoder waits at most 50 ms for queue room,
then skips new camera frames while preserving already encoded samples. There is
no shared recorder write lock in the captured stacks. Increasing queues would
only cover a bounded longer pause and cost memory; disabling or lengthening sync
would change durability/retained-sample assumptions. Neither was changed blindly.

**Least disruptive owner comparison:** keep USB, the same three sources, bitrate,
frame rate and GPU settings. If the owner wants to test, change **Settings →
Storage → Video storage cap** from the observed 10 GB to 100 GB for a comparable
drive and export diagnostics. With the observed approximately 10 GB of footage,
that gives cap headroom without disabling low-free-space checks. It retains more
footage and merely postpones cap-driven cleanup until the larger cap is reached;
it is not a permanent contention fix or a guarantee of eliminating sync pauses.
Do not use cap 0 as an equivalent: 0 intentionally disables automatic video
deletion and recording stops when free space becomes low.

Potential code follow-ups require separate changes and validation: log inventory,
individual unlink and total sweep durations; coordinate the hourly and segment
cleanup entry points; evaluate pacing maintenance around active writer backlog.
Keep final lock/open-file rechecks, bounded queues, no-limit semantics and final
file sync intact. No runtime patch or new build/test claim in this investigation.
